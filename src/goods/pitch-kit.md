# The pitch kit for small studios

ACME Digital Goods, Leeds. Written after ninety pitches, most of which we lost, and the forty since, most of which we do not.

## 1. Scope before you price

A price without a scope is an invitation to argue. Before you name a number, write down:

- **The deliverables**, as nouns: a logo, six page templates, a pattern library, a two-week discovery.
- **The rounds**: how many revisions, and what happens after they are spent.
- **What you are not doing**: no copywriting, no photography, no maintenance. Say it plainly; clients respect it.

## 2. Price the work, not the hours

Three numbers to hold in your head:

1. **Your floor**: rent, tools, tax, and a wage you would not resign over, divided by the billable hours you actually have (about 60 percent of the week).
2. **The value**: what the project is worth to them — a rebrand before a funding round is not worth the same as one before a sleepy AGM.
3. **The market**: what studios of your size charge locally. Price above your floor, near the value, and say why in one sentence.

Never discount the fee. Reduce the scope instead: fewer templates, one round, a shorter discovery. The price holds and everyone knows what they bought.

## 3. The one-page pitch

Write it so a busy founder can read it in ninety seconds:

- **Line one**: what you will do and by when. "A three-page site for your residency, live by 14 March."
- **The middle**: the deliverables, the rounds, the exclusions, and one idea — the single thing you would do that they have not thought of.
- **The fee**: one number, one sentence of reasoning, and the payment terms: half up front, half on delivery.
- **The end**: two links, not ten.

## 4. The follow-up

Send one email five working days after the pitch. Not "just checking in". Give something instead:

> Subject: One thought on the Milltown brief
>
> We put the pitch together with the launch date in mind, and one thing we did not fit in: the press page will be the first thing journalists hit. We would add a template for it at the same fee. Either way, we are free to start on the 3rd.

That is the whole trick: every contact after the pitch gives value, states your availability, and asks for nothing.

## 5. When you lose

Reply and thank them, then ask one question: what tipped it? You will get a real answer about one pitch in four, and three honest answers will change your next ten pitches more than any course.
